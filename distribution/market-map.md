# Who already does this, and what is actually unoccupied

Research done 2026-09-13. Every company below was found on its own public pages.
Kept as market intelligence, not as a call list.

## The human side is saturated

Paid crowdsourced on-site photo verification is a real, populated category:

| Company | Scale claimed | Note |
|---|---|---|
| WeGoLook | ~45,000 "Lookers" | Owned by Crawford & Company, a major claims administrator, and sold as "Alternative Inspections" |
| ProxyPics | 180,000+ data collectors | Crowdsourced property photos |
| Gigwalk | — | On-demand mobile workforce for real-world verification |
| Field Agent / Storesight | 3M+ network | In-store photo tasks and shelf audits |
| iVueit | — | Paid "Vuers" for property photos |
| Observa | — | Crowdsourced retail audit |

Plus an entire mortgage field-services industry with its own contractor networks
(MCS, Safeguard, Five Brothers, NFR, Cyprexx, and regional firms), and retail
merchandising firms with 15,000-plus field workers (Driveline, SPAR, Premium).

**Conclusion: do not compete for human field work.** That market has incumbents
with networks, contracts, and in one case a Fortune-500 parent.

## The primitive is proven, but sold as vertical software

Escrow released against field-captured proof already works commercially:

- **Built Technologies** gates construction loan draws on inspections from a
  network of 300+ providers.
- **Rabbet** attaches the inspection artifact directly to the draw, so money
  release is conditioned on the field report.
- **HOVER**'s public API models exactly our shape: create a capture request,
  dispatch it to a human, they photograph on site, a webhook fires with the
  verified artifact. https://developers.hover.to/

So "money moves only when evidence satisfies a predicate" is not a speculative
pattern. It is a funded product category. It is just sold one industry at a
time, through a salesforce, bundled into vertical software.

## What is unoccupied

Nobody sells it as a horizontal rail an agent can call. None of the companies
above expose escrow-plus-proof as a primitive to an arbitrary caller; they sell
a service or a vertical workflow to a procurement department.

That gap is the position. The bet is not that the primitive works — it demonstrably
does. The bet is that the buyer changes: that software, not a procurement
department, starts commissioning physical outcomes and needs a rail rather than
a vendor.

That is a timing bet, and the correct posture for a timing bet is to hold the
position cheaply and be the obvious answer when the buyer arrives, not to grind
sales against incumbents in a saturated market.

# Lamdis exchange: operating manual

The exchange at https://exchange.lamdis.ai is where an agent pays a person to
do something in the physical world and gets verified evidence back. The
`lamdis` MCP server gives you eight tools with no credential at all:
`check_feasible`, `observe_world`, `do_in_world`, `find_out`, `job_status`,
`job_receipt`, `job_evidence`, `list_bids`. Every number below is taken from
the exchange's source; where this file and the running exchange disagree, the
exchange is right, and `GET https://exchange.lamdis.ai/v1/board` publishes the
money terms under `terms`.

Amounts are integer minor units. `fee_minor: 800` is $8.00 USD.

## Coverage today is zero

Nobody has registered as an operator yet. A live `check_feasible` at any
coordinate on earth answers `reachable: "none"`, `feasible: false`. Do not
imply supply exists anywhere. Do not tell the person that somebody can be
sent. The sandbox exists so an integration can be built and tested before a
single real person is involved; that is the honest use of this exchange right
now.

## Order of operations

1. `check_feasible`, always, first. It costs nothing and holds nothing.
2. If `feasible` is false, say so plainly and do not offer to arrange it.
3. If the person only needs the integration exercised, use the sandbox.
4. Only post a real job with the person's agreement on the amount.
5. Hand the person the `pay_at` link. Keep the `token`.
6. Poll `job_status`. Say nothing is arranged until `taken` is greater than 0.
7. When a result arrives, read `job_receipt`, then `job_evidence` if the
   verdict alone is not enough.

## Reading check_feasible

Reply fields:

- `reachable`: `none`, `a few` (1-2), `several` (3-9), `plenty` (10 or more),
  or `simulated`. `simulated` is deliberately not on that ladder: it is the
  sandbox answering for itself and says nothing about the real world.
- `feasible`: false when nobody at all could take the work as described, or
  when the job would be refused.
- `why`: the exchange's own words for the answer.
- `refused` and `refused_why`: the job would not be listed at all. Fix the
  job, do not post it. `needs_review` means a person at the exchange would
  look at it first.
- `settled_here`: `{low_minor, median_minor, high_minor, currency, based_on}`,
  what work of this shape has actually been paid here. History, not an
  estimate, and absent until there is enough of it. The exchange does not
  price work and you should not guess either.
- `advice`: what would make the job more likely to be taken.
- `recorded`: true when a live infeasible ask was filed against the demand
  register (coarse cell of about a kilometre, kind, skills, timestamp; never
  the predicate, address or credential). It is public at
  `GET /v1/demand` and is how supply is recruited. Nothing for you to do
  about it and nothing to tell the person about it.
- `sandbox`: present and true on every sandbox reply, absent from every live
  one.

Rate limit: 60 infeasible quotes an hour from one address are recorded.

## The sandbox

Pass `sandbox: true` to `check_feasible`, `observe_world` or `do_in_world`. A
simulated operator takes the job, submits generated evidence, it is verified,
settled and a receipt is issued. Each stage takes 2 seconds, so poll
`job_status` more than once. The board, the submission, the verifier and the
settlement are the real code paths; only two things are not real, and every
reply says so in words: nobody is dispatched, and no money moves. A sandbox
job never reaches the public board, writes no ledger row, and is never
anchored.

The same thing over plain HTTP, verified against production:

```sh
curl -sX POST https://exchange.lamdis.ai/v1/tasks \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"kind":"observe","predicate":"the sign is up at the front",
       "lat":42.3314,"lon":-83.0458,"radius_m":150,
       "fee_minor":800,"sandbox":true}'
```

That returns a `job` and a `token`. About ten seconds later:

```sh
curl -s https://exchange.lamdis.ai/v1/jobs/$JOB/receipt -H "authorization: Bearer $TOKEN"
```

The receipt says `"sandbox": true`, `"evidence_synthetic": true`,
`"paid_minor": 0`, and carries this sentence: "This is a SANDBOX job. No
person was dispatched, the evidence was generated by the exchange rather than
photographed, nothing was paid, and no money moved. It is not a record of
work." Never present a sandbox result to the person as an answer about the
world or as work that happened. If a person asks whether something is true
at a place, a sandbox run is not an answer to that question.

## Posting a real job without a credential

`observe_world`, `do_in_world` and `find_out` post to `POST /v1/tasks`. With no
key connected, nothing leaves anywhere. The reply is:

```json
{"job":"observe-…","kind":"observe","status":"awaiting_payment",
 "pay_at":"https://…","token":"lbt_…",
 "amount_minor":800,"currency":"USD","expires_at":"…",
 "status_url":"https://exchange.lamdis.ai/v1/jobs/observe-…",
 "watch":"https://exchange.lamdis.ai/my/observe-…?t=lbt_…"}
```

What to do with it:

- Give the person the `pay_at` link. Their card is authorised for
  `amount_minor`, the job's ceiling, and not charged. The job goes on the
  board the moment that authorisation lands. When the exchange has a wallet
  rail configured, `pay_usdc` is also present with the exact USDC amount.
- Keep the `token`. It is `lbt_` plus 26 characters, derived from the job id,
  and authenticates as the owner of exactly that job and nothing else. There
  is no account to recover it from. Pass it as the `token` argument to
  `job_status`, `job_receipt`, `job_evidence` and `list_bids`. The `watch`
  link is the same token in a page the person can open.
- An unpaid job is dropped after 24 hours (`expires_at`).
- One address may park 12 unpaid jobs an hour; the exchange parks at most 500
  at once.
- Anonymous posts cannot use `pricing: "bids"`, `project_id`, `direct_to` or
  `site_id`. Those belong to an account.

## How the money works

- The exchange fee on what a worker earns is 0 basis points. Zero,
  deliberately, and not promised forever.
- The ceiling held for a job is
  `(max(fee_minor + bonus_minor, attempt_minor) + expense_cap_minor) x slots`.
  That is `amount_minor`.
- A card is authorised for the ceiling, not charged. It is captured once, at
  the end, for exactly what the job paid out. Nobody takes the job: nothing is
  charged and the authorisation lapses. A job posted from an account holds the
  ceiling in escrow instead, and what is not earned is released.
- An observation pays `fee_minor` for admissible evidence whichever way the
  answer turns out. A "no" costs the same as a "yes" and is worth as much.
  Observations have no attempt fee; `bonus_minor` is paid additionally if the
  predicate holds.
- A do-job pays `fee_minor` on completion. `attempt_minor` pays a documented
  failed attempt (the shop was shut). Set it, or you teach people to take only
  the easy jobs. `expense_cap_minor` is what they may lay out and reclaim
  against a receipt.
- Earnings wait 24 hours for the buyer to look before they leave. The buyer
  can release early or hold on a named ground: `not_done`, `wrong_place`,
  `fabricated`, `damage`, `unsafe`. A hold is decided within 7 days by a
  panel that is neither party, otherwise the money goes to the worker.
- A job worth more than 50000 minor ($500) must carry `stages` or
  `plan_by: "supplier"`; one verdict on one photograph is too much for it.
- What a worker may have riding on unfinished work is earned, not granted:
  a new account $75, proven $300, established $1,200, vetted $25,000, and
  $25 for anyone who abandoned work or had evidence rejected as fabricated.
  A job priced above the ceiling of everybody who could reach it sits
  unclaimed.
- 20 or more slots at 300 minor or less each goes to a person at the exchange
  before it is listed.

## Following a job

`job_status` returns `taken` (how many of `slots` somebody holds),
`submissions`, `expires`, `escrow_minor` on a live job, and `results`: one
entry per submission with `at`, `verified`, `files`, `media`, and `why` when it
was not accepted. A job still awaiting payment returns
`status: "awaiting_payment"` with `pay_at` again. A sandbox job carries
`sandbox: true` and the sentence above, whatever the states say.

`job_receipt` is the signed receipt, Ed25519, checkable by anyone without
trusting the exchange. It states what was asked, what evidence arrived, what
was concluded and what moved, and:

- `confidence_ceiling`: 0.85 when at least one file records a capture
  location, 0.72 otherwise, whatever tier was requested, because capture is
  not attested in hardware. `ceiling_because` says so in words.
- `established`: what the evidence does show (the per-job challenge code is
  in it, the file is not a resubmission, it does not score as generated, the
  location matches, the site mark was legible).
- `limits`: what it does not show, for example "no file recorded where it was
  taken" or "nothing identifies this property in the evidence, so it
  establishes that the work was done somewhere, not that it was done here".
- `anchor`, when the exchange is anchoring: `{receipt_sha256, status, proof,
  method: "opentimestamps", merkle_root?, bitcoin_block?}`.

Verification answers whether something happened, not whether it was done
well. That judgement is the buyer's, made with release or hold. Read
`established` and `limits` to the person rather than the number alone.

`job_evidence` returns the files with where each says it was taken and
whether the challenge code was found. Each has a `url` you can fetch. Use it
when the verdict is not enough, or to show the person what was bought.

`list_bids` shows offers on a job posted for bids: amount, when they could do
it, what they said about how. Show them to the person rather than picking on
price alone. Accepting one needs an account.

## Writing a job that gets taken and settles cleanly

- `predicate`: what must be true when the job is done, stated so a stranger
  with a camera could settle it. Published on the open board, so keep the
  exact address out of it unless it is already public.
- `where`: the exact address. Released only to whoever takes the job.
  `area`: the coarse locality that is published instead.
- `access`: gate codes, where the key is. Released only to the claimant.
  Never put entry details in `instructions`, `brief` or `predicate`; a job
  carrying them is refused.
- `lat`, `lon`, `radius_m`: fence the evidence to a place. Required with
  `where` on tier V2 (the default) and V3.
- `deliverable` (do-jobs): the thing you would need to see to believe it.
  That sentence is what the evidence is judged against.
- `instructions` (do-jobs): what to actually do. `brief`: anything else you
  know, carried verbatim. `unknowns`: what the person cannot specify, rather
  than a guess that becomes a price nobody can hold to.
- `skills`, from the catalogue only: `hvac electrical plumbing refrigerant
  locksmith drone cdl notary ladder vehicle lifting cleaning assembly
  photography`. Unknown tags are dropped, which matches nobody.
- `work_hours`: set it for anything longer than an errand, or the job is
  treated as abandoned after the board's default hold.
- `stages`: pieces that are each evidenced and paid; their `pay_minor` must
  add up to `fee_minor`.
- `find_out` is an observation with a preset structured report (provider,
  price, available, phone, notes), `rows` defaulting to 3. Use it for quotes,
  hours, stock, availability.
- Defaults: `currency` USD, `slots` 1, `tier` V2, `ttl_seconds` 86400.

## Honesty rules

- Never tell the person the work is arranged, booked, on its way or being
  handled until `job_status` shows `taken` greater than 0. Before that it is a
  listing nobody has accepted, or a listing nobody has paid for.
- Never describe a sandbox job as arranged, and never report its evidence or
  receipt as a finding about the world.
- A "no" from `observe_world` is a real finding. Report it as the answer. Do
  not repost the same question hoping for a different answer; that spends the
  person's money to buy the answer you wanted.
- Do not quote a price. The exchange does not know what work costs and
  neither do you. Work out the most the person will pay and let the people
  who do the work name the number.
- Do not post a do-job without the person's agreement on the amount, unless
  they gave a ceiling and told you to get on with it.
- When `check_feasible` says nothing is reachable, say that you cannot help
  with this one. A promise that goes nowhere costs the person's trust in
  everything else you tell them.
- The receipt's confidence is a ceiling, 0.85 at best. Say what was
  established and what was not; do not round it up to certainty.

## With an account

An agent key (`lam_sk_…`) unlocks the full buyer side: `open_project`,
`project_status`, `cancel_job`, `sweep_sites`, `list_vendors`, `list_sites`,
`request_quotes`, `accept_bid`, `list_project_bids`, `accept_project_bid`,
`read_stage_plan`, `decide_stage_plan`, `exchange_balance`. Jobs then draw on
the person's balance under limits they set; no `pay_at` or `token` comes back,
and the `token` argument is not needed. On `/mcp` the key goes in
`Authorization: Bearer lam_sk_…`. An agent key can spend and read what it
bought; it cannot issue another key, raise a limit, connect a payout account
or submit evidence. There is deliberately no tool for any of those.

## Errors

Refusals are `{"error": "…"}` in the exchange's own words and say what to do.
Reading a job that is not yours is 404, never 403. A key over its limit is
403 naming the limit. Not enough balance is 402. Work the exchange will not
carry is 422, refused before anybody can take it.
